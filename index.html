Curriculum

Fifteen weeks, mapped out

Our 15-week curriculum is designed to help students build strong debate foundations, improve their argumentation skills, and gain confidence through hands-on practice. Every term follows the same four stages, each building on the last and ending in a checkpoint a coach signs off on — no student moves forward on paper alone, they have to do it in a round first.

15-week term 4 stages A checkpoint at every stage

Week 1 · Start here

Foundations

Learn the language of a round

  • Claim, warrant, impact — and why an argument without all three loses
  • How to flow: taking notes fast enough to answer everything
  • Speaking on a timer without racing or trailing off
Checkpoint: give a 2-minute speech from a flow
Weeks 1–3

Building a case

Write something worth defending

  • Framework: the standard you're asking the judge to vote on
  • Two contentions, researched and cut into evidence you understand
  • Line-by-line coach edits on your own draft, not a template
Checkpoint: a finished case you wrote yourself
Weeks 3–6

Refutation

Answer what was actually said

  • Attacking a warrant instead of repeating your own case louder
  • Cross-examination: asking questions that set up your rebuttal
  • Building blocks for the arguments you keep losing to
Checkpoint: win a round you were losing at the half
Weeks 6–9

Weighing & the final speech

Tell the judge why you won

  • Impact calculus: scope, probability, and what happens first
  • Crystallizing a whole round into two voting issues
  • Full mock tournament with outside judges in week 12
Checkpoint: a ballot that quotes your final speech
Weeks 9–12

Week 15 · Tournament ready

What every single week looks like

The four stages change what you're learning. This part never changes.

TeachOne skill, explained and demonstrated by your coach.
→
DrillTwenty minutes of repetition on that one skill alone.
→
RoundA judged round. Everybody speaks — nobody sits out.
→
BallotA written decision within 48 hours: what won, what lost, what to fix.
↻ and the one thing to fix becomes next week's drill
